01

Development of AI-Based Rainwater Purification System for Aeta Community

Mar 2025 — Apr 2026

Many families in the Aeta community of Barangay Milagrosa rely on water sources that aren't reliably safe, especially during rainy season. This project built a solar-powered system that treats rainwater through multimedia filtration, UV disinfection, and reverse osmosis, then checks its own work, a Raspberry Pi 5 and it reads pH, turbidity, and TDS in real time and runs a machine learning model to classify whether the output is actually safe to drink, all shown on a local dashboard that works without internet.

We validated it with a DENR-accredited lab using real rainwater samples. The results were the clearest proof it worked, color dropped from 25 CU down to 1 CU, and the treated water passed every parameter in the Philippine National Standards for Drinking Water, including zero detectable E. coli. It was a strong first check that a compact, well-designed system can deliver genuinely safe water in places without steady power or piped infrastructure.

02

Peltier-Based Atmospheric Water Generator

Nov 2024 — Mar 2025

Instead of purifying an existing water source, this project asked a different question: could you pull drinkable water straight out of humid air? We built a device that cools air below its dew point using Peltier modules and a copper coil, condensing water vapor into liquid, then runs that water through carbon filtration, UV treatment, and pH/turbidity/TDS sensing before it's approved for storage.

Getting there took real troubleshooting. Our first integration test showed the water harvesting stage wasn't producing enough, we solved it by adding a second Peltier module for extra cooling capacity. We also had leakage in the water processing stage, fixed with better sealant and hose clamps. After those fixes, every subsystem like harvesting, filtration, water quality sensing, and environmental monitoring tested successfully as an integrated system.

03

24-Hour Digital Logic Clock

Jun 2024

Rather than relying on modern microcontrollers, this project explored the fundamentals of digital circuitry by building a functional clock using only discrete logic gates and synchronous decade counters. We designed a digital timekeeping device that accurately measures hours, minutes, and seconds, automatically resetting after a full 24-hour cycle. The hardware architecture uses a CD4060 IC oscillator to generate the timing pulses, cascaded 74LS90 decade counters to process the time logic, and 7447 BCD-to-7-segment decoders to drive the LED displays.

Moving the design from Circuit Wizard to a physical product required significant troubleshooting during the manufacturing stage. Our biggest hurdle was the pre-sensitized PCB fabrication, early development attempts resulted in broken circuit traces, forcing us to iterate on our process multiple times to successfully etch a clean board. We also had to adapt our hardware design on the fly due to supply limitations, adjusting our plans when our preferred 4-inch cathode displays were unavailable. Ultimately, careful attention to IC pin configurations and rigorous continuity testing resulted in a perfectly integrated, functional digital clock.

04

AC/DC Multi-Output Power Supply

Sept 2023

For this Electrical Circuits lab project, my team built a power supply that converts 220V AC mains into three regulated DC outputs of 3V, 9V, and 15V while meeting a 3% ripple factor and 250 mA current output target. It's a foundational build, a bridge rectifier and filtering stage clean up the incoming AC, followed by separate regulation stages for each output rail, all laid out on a custom-designed and hand-etched PCB.

We validated the circuit at each stage by doing it first on a breadboard, then on the etched board then using a multimeter to confirm actual output against target voltages. It's a smaller-scope project than my later embedded work, but it's the one that got me comfortable with the fundamentals: rectification, filtering, and regulation that show up in almost every piece of hardware I've built since.
